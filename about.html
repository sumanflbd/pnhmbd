Padma Bridge Museum

Preserving Bangladesh's Biodiversity and Cultural Legacy

About the Padma Bridge Museum

The Padma Bridge Museum is an environmental initiative established as part of the Padma Multipurpose Bridge Project. Developed by the Bangladesh Bridge Authority under the Ministry of Road Transport and Bridges, the museum is implemented by the Department of Zoology, University of Dhaka.

This museum serves as a center for biodiversity conservation, education, and research. The Padma Bridge project significantly altered the local ecosystem, affecting the biodiversity around the Padma River and its adjacent regions. While some species declined, others were introduced through reforestation and new habitat creation. The museum documents these changes and provides a space for research and public awareness.

Mission & Objectives

  • Document and preserve the biodiversity of the Padma River ecosystem
  • Create awareness about biodiversity and environmental conservation
  • Support education and zoological research
  • Offer a recreational and educational experience for visitors

Technical Team

An expert team of 18 members led the development of the Padma Bridge Museum, including:

  • 10 part-time professors from Dhaka University's Department of Zoology
  • 4 field biologists
  • 1 part-time taxidermist
  • 1 assistant taxidermist
  • 1 research associate
  • 1 curator

Key Museum Development Activities

  1. Animal Specimen Collection & Validation
    Over 2,158 specimens from 1,423 species were collected and validated, showcasing biodiversity from the Padma River and beyond.
  2. Collection of Natural and Cultural Heritage
    Includes over 2,400 heritage items like nests, eggs, traditional boats, fishing tools, and memorabilia of the Padma Bridge construction.
  3. Specimen Processing and Preservation
    Uses advanced preservation methods including wet storage, pinning, and modeling to ensure long-term display.
  4. Specimen Display
    Many specimens are displayed in simulated natural environments, while others are preserved for research.
  5. Digital Database Development
    A comprehensive digital database catalogs all species and specimen information for easy retrieval and study.
  6. Community Awareness Programs
    Conducted at schools and Union Parishad offices to spread knowledge about local biodiversity and conservation.
  7. Document Digitization
    Every aspect of collection and display has been digitized, supporting research and museum operations.

A National Landmark in Conservation

The Padma Bridge Museum is envisioned as the largest and most advanced natural history museum in Bangladesh. It stands as a model for environmental stewardship, education, and scientific exploration, offering insight into the country’s ecological transformation.
